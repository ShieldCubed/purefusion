§ 1Antiproton-induced thermonuclear burn

A small (~1018) number of antiprotons annihilate at the centre of a compressed Li₂DT fuel sphere. The charged pions (3 π± per annihilation, E = 236 MeV each) deposit energy in the surrounding fuel by multiple Coulomb scattering. If the spark energy exceeds the critical Ec/κ2 and the temperature exceeds Tc, an outgoing spherical thermonuclear detonation wave propagates through the fuel.

§ 2Implosion geometry (collapsing Li₂DT shell)

The 1-kt antimatter-triggered thermonuclear device conceived by Gsponer-Hurni (1986, Figure 2 of the source PDF): one microgram of antihydrogen levitated in a microcryostat at the centre of a 100-gram Li₂DT sphere, imploded by eight chemical-explosive lenses. At peak compression (κ ≈ 30) the shell contacts the antihydrogen; annihilation triggers the outgoing thermonuclear detonation wave.

Configuration parameters

Antihydrogen pellet: ~1 µg, solid, levitated in a 1 mm cryostat at < 0.1 K, magnetically suspended at the geometric centre of the device.

Li₂DT shell: 100 g, radius ~cm at κ = 1, compressed to ~mm at κ = 30. Lithium breeds tritium in situ via the 6Li(n,α)T reaction.

Chemical-explosive lenses: 8 sectors arranged octahedrally for spherical-implosion uniformity at the <1% level required for symmetric burn ignition.

Spark conditions (after compression)

Spark radius Rs: 0.07 cm at κ = 30 (Li₂DT).

Critical temperature Tc: 13.6 keV (Li₂DT) or 4 keV (DT).

Critical energy Ec: 3×1026 keV (Li₂DT) or 5×1025 keV (DT) — both scale as 1/κ2.

Antiproton count: ~1018 at κ = 30 (Gsponer-Hurni 1986); ~1017 with U-238 seed (Shmatov 2005).

§ 3Plasma-jet directed-energy weapon (DEW)

A shaped thermonuclear microexplosion produces, by hydrodynamic focusing, a collimated hypervelocity plasma jet. The principle is identical to a conventional shaped charge, except the energy source is a fusion burn rather than a chemical explosion. Jet velocity: 100-1000 km/s. Jet temperature: keV range. Range: 100-1000 km in vacuum (set by jet divergence and solar-wind interaction).

Performance envelope (conceptual)

Yield range: 0.1-1 kt TNT-equivalent per shot (micro explosion regime).

Jet velocity: 100-1000 km/s, set by fusion-burn temperature and shaped-charge geometry.

Jet temperature: keV range, set by fusion-product energy partition.

Effective range: 100-1000 km in space (set by jet geometric divergence and interaction with residual solar-wind plasma).

Open-literature status

Original reference: Gsponer & Hurni (1986), Section 7: “directed energy weapons applications may include the trigger of thermonuclear plasma jets.”

Underlying physics: studied in Russian literature on cumulative jets from thermonuclear microexplosions and U.S. SDI-era nuclear-explosion-pumped-laser literature.

Current TRL: 2 (concept formulation). No public-source engineering study gives a working design.

§ 4Arms-control and non-proliferation context

The technologies visualized above are at the intersection of six regimes (CTBT, NPT, IAEA safeguards, civilian fusion research, antimatter production, defense R&D) none of which provides a single verification gate that comprehensively covers them. The PDF report (Chapter 7) elaborates the policy options proposed in the open literature: no-first-pure-fusion-weapon declarations, threshold-yield declarations for ICF/MTF facilities, and safeguarding of large antimatter-production facilities above a stated rate.
